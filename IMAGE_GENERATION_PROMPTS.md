# Q Workwear — five images

Generate one opening image and four collections. Reuse these five throughout the website. The images describe garment styles; they are not documentary evidence of Q Workwear’s premises, people, customers or completed orders.

## Art direction — paste before every prompt

Create a photorealistic editorial photograph for a workwear collection. Quiet, contemporary, product-led. Directional daylight from a large window; clean highlights with detail in the shadows. Natural, colour-accurate fabric with visible weave, believable seams, correct buttonholes and realistic weight. Restrained charcoal, navy, soft white and muted red. The atmosphere comes from light, framing and material, not orange/teal grading, fog or heavy grain. New, clean, carefully prepared garments, not distressed clothing. One clear subject; no decorative clutter. No text, logos, watermarks, invented certifications or identifiable faces. No illustration, CGI, plastic fabric, impossible tailoring or melted hardware. Preserve the central subject with breathing room for both portrait and landscape crops.

Generate the corporate image first. Attach that result as a visual reference when making the others and ask for consistent lighting, contrast, palette and realism—not identical composition.

## 1. Opening image — craft in close-up

**Save as:** `public/images/showcase/hero-atelier-table.webp`  
**Frame:** 16:9 landscape; useful detail remains inside the central square.  
**Used on:** Home and About.

> A close, cinematic photograph of dark-skinned adult hands guiding tailor’s shears through a length of charcoal woven fabric on a plain matte cutting table. This is an anonymous maker, not a portrait of the business owners. Anatomically correct hands, a plausible grip and a mechanically coherent pair of shears. A fine chalk cutting line, the raised edge of the cloth and a folded navy fabric length create a simple diagonal composition. Only one tool besides the shears: a small piece of tailor’s chalk. Soft directional window light from the left traces the cloth weave and the knuckles. Focus on the fabric edge and cutting action, with a softly receding background. Keep the working action around the centre so both a wide panoramic website crop and a near-square mobile crop remain readable. No room-identifying signs or fabricated workshop branding. No added text.

## Review and export

- Check hands, collars, sleeves, buttons, pockets and zippers at full size. Regenerate mistakes; do not rely on a small preview.
- Test both portrait and landscape crops before accepting a collection image. The garment must remain identifiable.
- For the opening image, aim for at least 2000 pixels wide; for each collection, at least 1400 pixels wide. Use the best native export available, without invented upscaling detail.
- Export WebP, keep the filenames above, and retain the original files separately. No extra images are required for individual catalogue rows.
- Keep each exact prompt and generation source in the asset records. Check the applicable service terms and rights before publishing. Generated images must not be described as completed client work.
